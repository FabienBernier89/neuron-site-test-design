This language pair in practice

English is not an official language of the Swiss Confederation. Federal acts are authoritative in German, French and Italian; their English versions are translations published for information only, with no legal force. An English document therefore helps you understand and decide. It can never be the basis for an interpretation before a Swiss authority.

No authoritative version

English translations of federal acts are provided for information only and have no legal force. The reference text remains the German, French or Italian version. This caveat appears on the English translations published by the Confederation.

What gets converted

AGcompany limited by shares

Companies

In the Confederation's texts, Aktiengesellschaft is rendered as company limited by shares, not corporation. The abbreviation AG stays in the company name, which is never translated. A company name entered in the commercial register keeps its registered form.

Betreibung

Concepts without an equivalent

Betreibung is not debt collection in the common law sense, and a Verfügung is not a common law decision. The accepted approach is to keep the Swiss term and explain it once.

trust

The reverse trap

Consideration, trust, estoppel, discovery: these concepts have no counterpart in Swiss law. Using them to render a Swiss concept creates a mistranslation that the reader does not notice.

Art. 104 OR

Legal references

An article citation is left as it stands: Art. 104 OR or Art. 104 CO, followed by the full title of the Act. Inventing an English abbreviation makes the source impossible for the recipient to find.

Frequently asked questions about German to English translation

Does an English translation of a Swiss act have legal force?

No. English is not an official language of the Swiss Confederation. Federal acts are authoritative in German, French and Italian; the English versions are published for information only and cannot be relied on before a Swiss authority. For a binding citation, quote the German, French or Italian version, with its translation where needed.

Should Swiss terms be translated or kept?

Both, in that order. The Swiss term is kept, explained at its first occurrence, then reused as it is. That is the only way to render Betreibung or Verfügung without turning them into common law concepts that do not cover the same ground.

How should company names and abbreviations be handled?

They are not translated. A company entered in the commercial register as an AG or GmbH keeps that form throughout the document. Only the legal form may be explained once, for example company limited by shares for Aktiengesellschaft.

Are English translations less accurate than those in other pairs?

English is one of the four native languages of LexMachina, which is trained on Swiss legal and financial texts. What changes is not the quality of the language but the status of the text produced: it is for information only, because Swiss law is not written in English.

Can the document stay in Switzerland when the recipient is abroad?

Yes. Where processing takes place depends on the engine chosen, not on the recipient. With LexMachina or in Highly sensitive content mode, storage and processing stay in Switzerland only. You then send the translated file through your own channels.
