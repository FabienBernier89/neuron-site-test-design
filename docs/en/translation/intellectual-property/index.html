The acts a filing cites every day

Swiss intellectual property law is spread over four acts, one per type of right, plus the applicable international treaties. All are published in German, French and Italian, which gives a verifiable basis for the equivalents.

Translating a patent or a trade mark, in practice

In intellectual property, translating means producing a text that itself defines the extent of a right: the claims of a patent, the list of goods of a trade mark, the subject matter of a copyright assignment. The basic concepts are set by the Swiss acts on patents, trade marks, designs and copyright, published in the three official languages.

IP rights and filings

  1. Patent application

    The claims and the description must remain consistent with each other. A Full review by a legal translator checks every claim.

  2. Trade mark filing

    The list of goods and services determines the protection. An approximate term can narrow or widen the filing without anyone intending it.

  3. Licence agreement

    A licence agreement combines contract law and the law of IP rights: it brings together the terms of the Code of Obligations (CO) and those of the IP acts.

  4. Opposition and action

    Submissions filed with the authority or the court go through a Full review, or Double review & certified translation when an attestation is required.

Frequently asked questions on IP translation

Is a machine translation enough for a filing?

Not for the text you file. Machine translation prepares the work and helps you understand the prior art or an opposition filed by the other side. But a right you will assert against third parties justifies a Full review by a legal translator, or even Double review & certified translation when an attestation is required.

How do I keep the same terminology across a whole portfolio?

Fix the terms you choose in your own terminology resources, which Fast lookup CHnell shows alongside its 30 sourced domains. Then let projects in Standard confidentiality feed a translation memory that only your organisation can reuse.

Can an invention that has not been filed yet be translated safely?

Before publication, an invention is a trade secret, protected in particular by Article 162 of the Swiss Criminal Code (SCC). Highly sensitive content mode requires storage and processing in Switzerland only, and nothing is reused in a translation memory. This suits a text that is still confidential.

Are scanned technical documents supported?

Yes. The PDF to Word tab converts a scanned PDF into an editable Word document before translation, which is common for prior art documents and for annexes in opposition proceedings. The .pdf, .doc, .docx, .xls, .xlsx, .ppt and .pptx formats are accepted, up to 25 files of 50 MB per upload.

Which languages can I use for an extension abroad?

German, French, Italian and English are native in every pair, and the platform covers 30 languages in total. The Swiss reference, however, remains the German, French or Italian text of the applicable acts, which the concordancer shows with its source.

Can I compare several wordings of a claim?

Yes. The Alternatives panel offers up to four translations of the same passage, each labelled with the engine that produced it. This helps when two wordings are linguistically correct but do not have the same technical scope. The legal expert decides, and the chosen term can be fixed for the whole portfolio.
