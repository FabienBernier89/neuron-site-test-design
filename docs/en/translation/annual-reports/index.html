The acts an annual report cites every day

Swiss accounting law sits in the Code of Obligations, which sets the minimum structure of the accounts and the audit thresholds. Listed companies and collective investment schemes add their own disclosure obligations.

Its Title 32 governs commercial accounting, the presentation of accounts and consolidation

Translating an annual report, in practice

Translating a Swiss annual report means handling two things at once: a freely drafted management commentary, and financial statements in which every item has a name set by the accounting law of the Code of Obligations. The balance sheet, the profit and loss account and the notes have official labels in German, French and Italian, and publication binds the company.

Closing documents

  1. Annual report

    The narrative part is machine-translated first, then goes through a Full review: it is the most widely read and quoted document outside the company.

  2. Annual accounts

    The balance sheet, the profit and loss account and the notes use the labels of the Code of Obligations. Those are the labels that must appear, not their everyday equivalents.

  3. Audit report

    The external auditor's report follows very stable wording. The translation memory carries it over from one closing to the next.

  4. Notes to the accounts

    The notes cite rules and methods. A Light review is enough for an internal document, a Full review for publication.

Frequently asked questions on translating financial statements

Are Excel tables translated without breaking the structure?

Yes. Excel files (.xls and .xlsx) are supported like other office formats and come back in their original format, with the table structure preserved. For a complex file, upload the native format rather than a PDF export.

Which accounting labels should I use?

Those of Title 32 of the Code of Obligations, which names the balance sheet, the profit and loss account and the notes in the three official languages. Market usage exists, but a published report is better served by the statutory vocabulary, which the concordancer shows with its source.

Is a report that is not yet published protected?

Storage is on Swiss servers in every case. For a report under embargo, Highly sensitive content mode also requires processing in Switzerland only, with no reuse of the content in a translation memory. Before anything is sent, engines hosted abroad display the Attorney-Client privilege notice, which reminds users of your organisation's policy.

Does everything need human review?

Rarely. The management commentary, which will be read and quoted, justifies a Full review. Highly standardised notes to the accounts can make do with a Light review. Even an Internal review by your teams may be enough: the project workflow estimates its time from the number of pages detected.

How do I save time from one closing to the next?

Reuse the translation memory built from projects in Standard confidentiality: unchanged passages are not retranslated, and the work focuses on what is new in the financial year. The memory stays reusable only by your organisation. The external auditor's report, which is very stable, benefits in particular.

In which languages can I publish?

German, French, Italian and English are native in every pair, which covers the needs of a Swiss report. The platform supports 30 languages in total for publications aimed at foreign investors. You choose the languages document by document in the project workflow.
