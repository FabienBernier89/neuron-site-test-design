This language pair in practice

Federal acts are adopted and published in German, French and Italian, and all three versions are equally authoritative. Translating from German into French is therefore not a matter of inventing an equivalent. It means finding the word the legislature itself used in the French version of the same article.

Three versions, equal force

A federal act exists in German, French and Italian, and all three texts are authoritative. Translation means finding the official French version, not producing a new one. Classified Compilation of Federal Legislation: SR in German, RS in French and in Italian.

What gets converted

ORCO

Abbreviations to convert

OR becomes CO, ZGB becomes CC, StGB becomes CP, SchKG becomes LP, IPRG becomes LDIP. A German abbreviation left in a French text stands out on first reading.

BGEATF

Federal case law

A decision cited as BGE in German is cited as ATF in French and DTF in Italian, with the same volume, part and page number. The reference is converted, not translated.

ßss

Swiss spelling

The German written in Switzerland has no ß and always uses ss. A document containing ß does not come from Switzerland, which often changes the applicable law. A useful clue for spotting a foreign document in a batch of several hundred pages.

15'000

Figures and amounts

Thousands are separated with an apostrophe: 15'000. The format must survive translation, in the body text as well as in tables and appendices full of figures.

Frequently asked questions about German to French translation

Is Swiss legal German really different from the German used in Germany?

Yes, and not only in spelling. Switzerland does not use ß, thousands are separated with an apostrophe and quotation marks are guillemets. Above all, the vocabulary refers to different laws: SchKG, OR and ZGB have no direct counterpart in Germany. An engine trained on texts from Germany produces terms that do not exist in Swiss law.

How are abbreviations of Acts handled?

They are converted, never copied. OR becomes CO, ZGB becomes CC, StGB becomes CP, SchKG becomes LP and IPRG becomes LDIP. Where the context leaves any doubt, the full title of the Act is written out instead of an approximate abbreviation.

Are case law references converted too?

Yes. A German BGE is cited as ATF in French, with the same volume and page. The Federal Gazette is cited as BBl in German and FF in French, and the Classified Compilation changes from SR to RS. These conversions are mechanical, but they are what makes a written submission citable before a French-speaking authority.

Does a long document keep its layout?

Yes, the translation is delivered in the original format. Text and document translation accepts up to 25 files of 50 MB per upload, in .pdf, .doc, .docx, .xls, .xlsx, .ppt and .pptx format. The PDF to Word tab turns a scan into an editable document before processing.

Can the text stay in Switzerland from start to finish?

Yes. Projects are stored on Swiss servers in every case, and Highly sensitive content mode or the LexMachina engine also guarantees processing in Switzerland only. If a team member chooses a third-party engine hosted abroad, the Attorney-Client privilege notice appears before anything is sent.
