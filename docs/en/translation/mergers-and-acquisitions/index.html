Translating a transaction, in practice

Translating a merger or an acquisition means handling two very different sets of documents. On one side, thousands of pages of due diligence to understand quickly. On the other, a handful of instruments that bind the parties, from the merger agreement to the general meeting resolutions. The vocabulary comes from the Mergers Act and the Code of Obligations, in their three official versions.

Transaction documents

  1. Data room

    The batch goes into File translation and comes back in its original format. The team reads instead of deciphering, with no copying and pasting from one document to another. Up to 25 files of 50 MB per upload, in .pdf .doc .docx .xls .xlsx .ppt .pptx formats.

  2. Due diligence report

    The report is written in one language and read in another by the investment committee. A Light review is enough for an internal document.

  3. Merger agreement

    The instrument that binds the parties goes through Full review or Double review & certified translation, with the delivery time shown before you approve the quote.

  4. Corporate resolutions

    The merger resolution, the external auditor's report and the register entries use the labels of the Mergers Act and the CO, not their synonyms.

  5. Merger control filing

    A concentration subject to notification is described in the language of the authority concerned, with the vocabulary of the Cartel Act.

Frequently asked questions on M&A translation

Can a data room be translated without the documents leaving Switzerland?

Yes. Storage is on Swiss servers in every case, and processing stays in Switzerland with LexMachina or in Highly sensitive content mode. If a team member chooses an engine hosted abroad, the Attorney-Client privilege notice appears before sending and reminds them of your organisation's policy: pseudonymisation first.

How many documents can I upload at once?

Up to 25 files of 50 MB each per upload in Text and document translation, in .pdf, .doc, .docx, .xls, .xlsx, .ppt and .pptx formats. For a larger batch, for tracking by batch or for human review, start from Translation projects.

Does every document in a deal need human review?

No, and that is the point of keeping the two tasks apart. Documents read for understanding go through machine translation. The instruments that bind the parties, starting with the merger agreement and the corporate resolutions, go through Full review or Double review & certified translation, chosen document by document.

Do the deal documents feed a shared database?

Projects in Standard confidentiality can feed a translation memory that only your organisation can reuse. Projects in Highly sensitive content mode are never reused in a memory, which suits deals that are still confidential. You choose the mode for each project when you create it.

How should restructuring vocabulary be translated?

With the terms of the Mergers Act, which distinguishes four transactions: merger, demerger, conversion and transfer of assets and liabilities. Each has an official name in German, French and Italian, which you can look up in context in Fast lookup CHnell with its reference.

What about documents in a less common language?

The platform covers 30 languages, with German, French, Italian and English native in every pair. A foreign subsidiary whose contracts are drafted in another language therefore stays readable for the deal team. Documents come back in their original format, whatever the source language.
