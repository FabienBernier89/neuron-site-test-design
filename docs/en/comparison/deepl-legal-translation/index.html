Corrext and DeepL. The same engine, within a framework that protects professional secrecy

DeepL Pro is a very good general-purpose translation engine, and it is available in Corrext. This page does not try to turn you away from it. It sets out what DeepL Pro does well, then exactly where the difference lies for a Swiss legal matter.

Request a demo

The short answer

Yes, DeepL Pro can translate a legal text: it is a fast, fluent engine.

In Corrext, it remains available, but under your organisation's governance. The difference is not fluency. It lies in where data is processed, in Swiss legal terminology and in the review chain.

  • Where processing happens, decided by you

    Storage on Swiss servers in every case; processing exclusively in Switzerland with LexMachina or in Highly sensitive content mode, in German, French, Italian and English.

  • Swiss terminology, with sources

    The CHnell concordancer shows the term in aligned segments from the Federal Gazette, fedlex.admin.ch or other official sources, with its domain.

  • The rest of the work

    Alternatives compared per engine, Light, Full or Double review ordered in the tool, quote and delivery time, certified translation with notarisation or apostille.

What DeepL Pro does well

It would be dishonest to claim otherwise: DeepL Pro is one of the best general-purpose translation engines on the market, and many legal experts use it every day to good effect.

  • Fluency

    Its output reads naturally, even in long sentences with subordinate clauses, which are the daily fare of a statute or a contract.

  • Formats

    It translates Office documents and PDFs while keeping most of the layout, which saves rebuilding a document by hand.

  • Glossaries

    It lets you define your own term pairs, so that a specific word is translated the same way from one document to the next.

  • A public commitment on data

    DeepL states that its Pro customers' texts are deleted after translation and are not used to train its models. We have no reason to doubt it.

Eleven criteria, verifiable on both sides

What we say about DeepL Pro is limited to what is public and stable: a general-purpose translation engine, infrastructure located outside Switzerland, no integrated legal review chain, no Swiss law concordancer with sources. On everything else, DeepL Pro does what it says it does, and does it well.

Should a law firm give up DeepL?

No, and banning it does not work: shadow translation starts again the next day, in a tab nobody sees. Corrext starts from the opposite premise. DeepL Pro remains available, alongside LexMachina, Azure OpenAI GPT and Claude, but choosing it displays the Attorney-Client privilege notice at the moment of sending. The notice restates your organisation's policy: pseudonymisation of the document, sensitive matters excluded. Your teams keep the tool they like, and your professional secrecy keeps its framework.

Frequently asked questions about DeepL and legal translation

Can I use DeepL for a document covered by professional secrecy?

The issue is not the quality of the engine. It is the transfer of the document outside Switzerland. In Corrext, choosing DeepL Pro triggers the Attorney-Client privilege notice, which restates your organisation's policy: the document must be fully pseudonymised, and these engines are not used for sensitive matters. For a document that has not been pseudonymised, choose LexMachina or Highly sensitive content mode, both of which require processing exclusively in Switzerland. The rule that binds you remains that of your firm and your supervisory authority.

Does DeepL process my data in Switzerland?

No. DeepL is a German company and its infrastructure is located outside Switzerland. In Corrext, DeepL Pro is therefore classed among the third-party engines hosted abroad and triggers the Attorney-Client privilege notice. For storage and processing exclusively in Switzerland, choose LexMachina or turn on Highly sensitive content mode, available in German, French, Italian and English.

Does Corrext replace DeepL?

No: Corrext can use DeepL Pro. You choose the engine each time you send a text, alongside LexMachina, Azure OpenAI GPT and Claude, and the Alternatives panel compares up to four suggestions, each labelled with its engine. What Corrext adds is not one more engine. It is the framework around it: Swiss storage, the notice before sending, the CHnell concordancer, human review ordered in the tool, the quote and certification.

What does an engine trained on Swiss law add?

LexMachina is trained on more than 15 million Swiss multilingual legal and financial segments. In practice, it knows the country's abbreviations and conventions: CO and OR, LB and BankG, LDIP and IPRG (the French and German abbreviations of the Code of Obligations, the Banking Act and the Federal Act on Private International Law), the Swiss spelling with ss, and the Federal Supreme Court rather than a foreign court. That does not make it infallible: no engine is a source of law, which is why Corrext shows the alternatives and the concordancer.

Does DeepL Pro keep my texts?

DeepL states publicly that texts translated by its Pro customers are deleted after translation and are not used to train its models. It is a serious commitment, and we have no reason to doubt it. For a matter covered by professional secrecy, the open question is not retention. It is the transfer of the document outside Switzerland, and the absence of any warning at the moment of sending.

How do I check a Swiss legal term after a translation?

In Corrext, highlight the word in the source text and click Fast Lookup. The CHnell concordancer displays aligned segments from official Swiss sources, with the domain and source of each occurrence, and a Show in context link. Verzugszins, for example, returns more than 300 results and Bankgeheimnis more than 400. This traceability is what a translation engine alone does not provide.
