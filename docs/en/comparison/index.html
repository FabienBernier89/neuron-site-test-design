How we compare

These pages are written by Neur.on, the publisher of Corrext. So we do not claim to be neutral: we commit to being verifiable. Every row of every table covers a criterion you can check yourself, in both tools, without taking our word for it.

  • What we compare

    Where data is stored and where it is processed, whether a warning appears before a document is sent, Swiss legal terminology available in context, the handling of Swiss legal references, the ability to compare several engines, human review ordered in the tool, the quote and delivery time, certified translation, formats and deployment.

  • What we do not compare

    Prices: they depend on your volume, your languages and your review level, and a side-by-side price list would mean nothing out of context. Raw speed: all the engines cited translate in a few seconds, and the difference decides nothing. Quality ‘in general’: it cannot be measured on a single sentence.

  • What we say about others

    Only what is public and stable on the date of checking: the general-purpose nature of an engine, the location of its infrastructure, the absence of an integrated legal review chain, the absence of a Swiss law concordancer with sources.

    Where a point seemed uncertain to us, we qualified it rather than settling it.
  • What we say about Corrext

Frequently asked questions about these comparisons

Are these comparisons neutral?

No, and we would rather say so: they are written by Neur.on, the publisher of Corrext. That is precisely why they stick to criteria you can check yourself: where data is stored and processed, Swiss legal terminology in context, the handling of legal references, and whether there is a review chain, a quote and certification. We compare neither prices nor raw speed.

Can Corrext use DeepL Pro, Azure OpenAI GPT or Claude?

Yes. These engines are available in Corrext alongside LexMachina, and you choose one each time you send a text. Because they are hosted abroad, selecting one triggers the Attorney-Client privilege notice, which restates your organisation's policy: full pseudonymisation of the document. So we are not asking you to give up these engines. We suggest using them within a framework that protects your professional secrecy.

What are these comparisons based on?

Everything we say about Corrext has been observed in the application. What we say about other solutions is limited to what is public and stable. The translation examples are real, one-off outputs, never statistical measurements.
