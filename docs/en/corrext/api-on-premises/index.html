API and on-premises. Integrate the engine where the work already happens

Your legal experts should not have to change tools to translate. You call the engine trained on Swiss law from your document management system, your intranet or your own product. When your internal rules require it, it runs in your own infrastructure.

Scope an integration

Three ways to integrate Neur.on into your IT systems

Document management

Translate from the DMS

A document stored in your document management system goes to translation from its own screen. The target version comes back next to the original, in the same folder structure and under the same rights. Your legal experts do not leave the tool in which they already file their matters, and no copy goes to an ungoverned online service.

Intranet

A translation service for everyone

Translation becomes a building block of your portal: an internal form, an upload page, a button in an approval workflow. Teams that would never open a translation platform get access where they work, with the languages and engines you have decided on.

LegalTechs and publishers

The engine in your product

You develop legal software and your Swiss clients ask for their content to be translated. Rather than train an engine, you call ours from your product: the feature is yours, the infrastructure and Swiss terminology are ours. The scope and the brand displayed are set out in the contract.

One call, the same settings as in the interface

What you choose on screen in Corrext, you also choose in a call: source language, target language, engine and area of law. This is how an integration follows the same governance as the interface, instead of creating a parallel channel without rules.

  • Source and target language, from the languages on the platform
  • Choice of engine, and therefore of where processing takes place
  • Area of law, for the expected terminology
  • Text or file, depending on what your system sends

Frequently asked questions about the API and on-premises deployment

Do you offer an API to integrate translation into our tools?

Yes. A REST API lets you call the engine from your own systems: document management, intranet, business workflows. We do not publish open technical documentation. The interface contract, authentication and scope of calls are set during scoping, with your IT department and our technical team.

Can we deploy the solution in our own infrastructure?

On-premises deployment is possible for the most sensitive environments, that is, when your internal rules require that nothing leaves your network. It is a full integration project, scoped with your IT department: architecture, sizing, updates and operation are defined before any commitment.

Can our access rights be imported from SharePoint or Active Directory?

This is possible as part of an integration project. Access rights can be synchronised with your directory and your document spaces, so that everyone finds in Corrext the same scope they already have in your systems. This synchronisation is not a self-service setting in the web interface: it is set up with your IT department.

Can we integrate the engine into our own product, on a white-label basis?

This is the use case for LegalTech companies and legal publishers: translation becomes a feature of your product, called via API, without your users changing tools. The functional scope, the brand displayed and the terms are set out in the contract during scoping.

Where is data processed during an API call?

The same rules apply as in the interface: storage takes place on Swiss servers in all cases. Processing takes place exclusively in Switzerland with LexMachina or in Highly sensitive content mode, which is available for German, French, Italian and English. The third-party engines, DeepL Pro, Azure OpenAI GPT and Claude, are hosted abroad and trigger the Attorney-Client privilege notice, which reminds users of your organisation's policy. With an on-premises deployment, processing stays within your own infrastructure.
