Data sovereignty. Where exactly your document goes, and who can read it

“Hosted in Europe” means nothing to a lawyer bound by professional secrecy, or to a bank bound by banking secrecy under Article 47 of the Banking Act (BankA). Here is where your document is stored, where it is processed and what remains of it afterwards, mode by mode and engine by engine.

Request a demo

What puts a confidential document at risk

  1. Generic tool

    An online tool that processes the text outside Switzerland

    A generic translation tool processes data with a foreign operator. Any paragraph pasted into it leaves Swiss territory.

  2. US law

    The CLOUD Act follows the provider, not the server

    The US CLOUD Act applies to providers subject to US jurisdiction, wherever they store the data. Hosting in Switzerland with such a provider makes no difference.

  3. Professional secrecy

    A duty of secrecy that rules out exposing the case file

    Lawyers' professional secrecy and banking secrecy are protected by criminal law. Before you send a document covered by either, you need to know where the text will be processed.

Three confidentiality regimes, three ways to use Corrext

The document stays in Switzerland

A court filing identifies the client from its first line: pseudonymising it costs more than translating it. With Highly sensitive content mode or the LexMachina engine, the question no longer arises. Storage and processing stay in Switzerland, and nothing feeds a memory in sensitive mode.

A rule backed by criminal penalties

Disclosing a secret entrusted to a bank is a criminal offence under Article 47 of the Banking Act (BankA), and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA) expects documented control of the processing chain. Sensitive mode gives the auditor a three-line answer: one country, one entity, one certification.

Documents that are not public yet

Before publication, a draft law, a report or a ruling cannot pass through a service whose country of processing is unknown. Corrext lets you translate it into German, French, Italian or English without it leaving Swiss territory, and keeps track of who has had access to it.

Frequently asked questions on security and data sovereignty

Where is my data hosted?

On Swiss servers. Corrext runs on Infomaniak infrastructure in Switzerland. The projects and files you upload are stored there, whichever confidentiality mode and translation engine you choose.

Is my data processed in Switzerland?

Storage is in Switzerland in every case. Processing takes place exclusively in Switzerland when Highly sensitive content mode is switched on, or when you translate with LexMachina. In Standard mode with a third-party engine, the text is processed by an engine hosted abroad: the application warns you before anything is sent.

Can I use DeepL or ChatGPT for a matter covered by professional secrecy?

Corrext does not block it technically: it reminds you of your organisation's policy at the moment you choose the engine. The Attorney-Client privilege notice then appears. Under that policy, only fully pseudonymised documents may be sent to an engine hosted abroad, and never a high-profile or price-sensitive matter. For a document covered by professional secrecy, the safe choice is LexMachina or Highly sensitive content mode.

Are my documents used to train a model?

No. LexMachina is trained on public Swiss legal and financial texts, not on your files. In Standard mode, your projects feed a translation memory that only you can use, as defined in Corrext. In Highly sensitive content mode, no memory is fed at all.

What happens to a Highly sensitive project?

It is stored and processed exclusively in Switzerland, and its translated content is not reused in the translation memories of later projects. The mode is available for German, French, Italian and English. You turn it on with a switch in Text and document translation, and select it when you create a translation project.

Is Neur.on subject to the CLOUD Act?

No. Neur.on AI Solutions SA is a Swiss company based in Fribourg, and the data is hosted in Switzerland on Swiss infrastructure. The US CLOUD Act, which targets providers subject to US jurisdiction, does not apply here. If you yourself choose a third-party engine hosted abroad, however, the text you send it is subject to the law that applies to that provider.

Can we run the platform on our own servers?

Yes. Corrext can be deployed on-premises, in your own environment, when your security policy or your regulator requires it. A REST API also lets you call the translation function from your DMS, your intranet or your existing workflows. The right option depends on your infrastructure and your obligations: we can discuss it during the demo.
